# Frozen blind visual review

## Scope and limits

This report was frozen with candidates identified only as A and B. I inspected the actual primary, secondary, and interior reference PNGs, plus the primary, secondary, interior, and heldout PNGs for each candidate. The review instructions and reference brief supplied the intended scene. No candidate source, authoring logs, identity mapping, or other review informed these findings.

These are qualitative observations of still images. The heldout view is used for spatial consistency, not comparison with an unavailable reference camera. Different offline and realtime lighting, indirect illumination, noise, and tone mapping prevent treating pixel appearance as a direct renderer-correctness test. Geometry/style differences below are visible mismatches, not established implementation defects. Source correctness, editability, and moving articulation are outside what these images can establish.

## Candidate A

### Composition/construction: partly satisfied

The primary view preserves the recognizable arrangement very well: warm hall at the back left, pale apron, teal cart and barrels by the small door, crane and suspended ring beside the portal, exhaust at the right wall, and pipe stock farther right. The relative scale of the tall portal, crane, roof and plinth is convincing. The secondary view retains the reference's broad unbroken side wall and frontal court organization. In the interior view the mezzanine runs behind the arch, with the lathe beneath it; the open front roof and covered rear portion remain legible.

There are local differences rather than a missing major scene element. The cargo beside the cart reads as a row of narrow upright slabs/crates, whereas the reference presents chunkier boxes. The lathe has a particularly dark, bulky headstock and a longer-looking horizontal bed in the frontal view; its balance of dark headstock, teal body and supports differs from the reference. The open-roof members read mostly as broad dark crossing/sloping strips; the exact silhouette and layering do not match the reference. These deviations do not prevent recognizing the intended construction.

### Spatial consistency/occlusion: satisfied for the visible evidence

The opposite oblique views and lower frontal view support one coherent scene. The portal reveals different parts of the lathe and staircase as expected. The arch jambs and façade hide interior contents at oblique angles. In the heldout view, the side opening exposes the stair and its diagonal members while the wall obscures the rest. The crane's narrow edge-on presentation hides most of the hanging ring in that view, a plausible change of occlusion rather than a disappearing assembly. The cart, barrels, lamp and stock rack retain believable positions relative to the hall and apron.

The heldout framing exposes substantial building depth and the roof's open front region. Nothing in these stills requires a camera-facing façade interpretation. This does not prove watertight construction, exact dimensions, correct transform hierarchy, or behavior after an edit.

### Curved/hollow forms: partly satisfied

The portal has a credible faceted semicircular crown and visibly encloses an opening. The hanging load reads as a thick annular object, with the background wall visible through its center. Stock-tube mouths have distinct outer rims and dark inner bores, most clearly in primary and heldout. The exhaust makes a readable rounded bend with faceted surface bands rather than an angular box corner.

The conspicuous weakness is the portal's inner left jamb: primary, interior and heldout show repeated horizontal striping and irregular edge breakup down the reveal, unlike the reference's quieter solid wall thickness. There is also fine dark breakup at the crown/trim boundary. These are appearance observations; their geometric or shading cause is not established.

The exhaust outlet in heldout reads as an almost uniform black circular face. I cannot distinguish a deeply shadowed opening from a closed dark face. The inlet is not exposed clearly enough to assess, and the stock tubes' full through-depth is similarly not demonstrated. Thus the requirement that both exhaust ends be genuinely open is unverifiable from these images, despite the convincing external pipe shape.

### Material/light intent: partly satisfied

The major color families remain readable: ochre architecture, sand trim, teal equipment, orange/saffron boom, dark steel and roof, neutral apron. Warm wall lamps and the cool portable fixture are visually distinct. The portable diffuser is a bright pale bulb beneath a separate dark hood; its pole and base remain opaque-looking. A broad cooler/brighter region is visible around the portable lamp on the apron.

Compared with the reference, warm lamp halos become brighter yellow-white centers, the sunlit wall becomes flatter and brighter, and much of the shaded steel/interior becomes nearly black. The reference's softer warm ambience and restrained pool transitions are not closely reproduced. Ground shadows around the rack and crane are much harder, with visibly stepped edges. A fine sawtooth dark fringe runs beneath the front fascia in the interior view and is visible from the oblique views too. The apron also contains some large flat tonal patches that stand out more than in the reference.

The general sun-shadow direction remains coherent across ground objects, and the crane's angular shadow on the wall is spatially plausible. Exact light falloff, exposure equivalence, metalness, and which individual fixture component casts each shadow cannot be confirmed from these stills. The appearance differences do not establish an engine defect.

## Candidate B

### Composition/construction: partly satisfied

The same scene is readily recognizable, with the hall, tall portal, open front roof, rear roof, mezzanine, staircase, lathe, cart, barrels, crane, ring, lamp, exhaust and stock rack all contributing in the expected places. Primary and secondary preserve the reference's overall staging, while interior places the lathe beneath the mezzanine and reveals substantial depth through the portal.

Visible local departures include the cargo boxes' broader, more separated forms and top-rail details, which differ from the reference's compact grouping. The roof frame has prominent upright/triangular subdivisions at the ridge, especially in interior and heldout, giving it a different silhouette from the reference roof members. The side-window surround reads as a heavy, continuous light rectangular frame in heldout; the reference's visible treatment is slimmer and less emphatic. The lathe has a boxier headstock body than the reference. None of these differences erases a major semantic part of the scene.

### Spatial consistency/occlusion: satisfied for the visible evidence

The portal consistently reveals the lathe, floor and mezzanine, with the stair appearing toward the far side in secondary and through the side opening in heldout. The façade and opening thickness occlude the interior appropriately as the view changes. The crane becomes a narrow stacked silhouette in heldout and obscures the suspended load. The rack is still beside the exhaust, while the service cart, barrels and lamp remain on their corresponding sides of the court.

The heldout view supports a volumetric hall with meaningful side-wall openings and a roofed rear region. No obvious disconnected façade or gross view-dependent relocation is visible. Exact dimensions, hidden construction, transform hierarchy and edit correctness remain unverifiable.

### Curved/hollow forms: partly satisfied

The portal crown reads as a continuous faceted arch, with a comparatively clean inner jamb in the frontal and heldout images. The suspended ring has substantial thickness and a visible central opening. Stock tubes present annular mouths with dark interiors; the exhaust bend has a coherent faceted curve.

As with A, the heldout exhaust outlet reads as a nearly flat black disk, without enough visible inner-wall detail to establish a hollow exit. The inlet and full stock-tube depth are not shown clearly enough to validate open-through construction. The ring and arch supply much stronger visual evidence of actual visible openings than the exhaust does. Some fine dark edge roughness remains around trim and other small boundaries, but the conspicuous ladder-like breakup on A's inner arch jamb is not present to the same degree here.

### Material/light intent: partly satisfied

The scene retains the intended warm architecture, cooler machinery, orange/saffron crane and pale apron. Warm wall lights remain distinguishable from the pale cool portable diffuser. Hood, bulb, pole and base are visually separable. The apron around the portable fixture is cooler/brighter than much of the surrounding plinth, although the precise source contribution and falloff cannot be isolated.

The warm wall-light hotspots are much more yellow-white and concentrated than the softer reference glow. Strong lit/shaded separation leaves steel bores and interior members very dark. The crane's teal base box is noticeably bright cyan-teal, particularly in interior and heldout, and attracts more attention than the darker box in the reference. The orange boom also looks lighter/paler on lit faces. The ground shadows remain sharp and visibly jagged around the rack/crane, and the front fascia carries the same conspicuous fine sawtooth fringe seen in A. Some tonal blocks on the apron are more obvious than the reference's softer surface treatment.

General shadow placement is coherent; precise fixture shadow participation and renderer/material correctness cannot be inferred. The deviations describe this image treatment rather than its cause.

## Frozen synthesis

Both candidates satisfy the visible cross-view spatial-consistency test and partly satisfy composition/construction, curved/hollow-form, and material/light-intent fidelity. Their broad reconstruction is substantially recognizable; the remaining concerns are local shape/style choices, edge/shadow presentation, and incomplete visual evidence of exhaust hollowness.

A's most distinctive visible concern is the striped inner arch reveal. B's arch reads cleaner, while its stronger roof subdivisions, heavy side-window surround and brighter cyan crane box are more conspicuous departures in other areas. Neither candidate supports a general superiority claim. The shared hard, jagged shadows and hotter lamp pools are larger presentation differences from the offline reference than many of the differences between A and B, but they do not by themselves identify a rendering defect.

Findings are frozen before any candidate identity reveal. No numeric score, productivity assessment, source audit, or causal engine judgment is made.
