# Frozen blind qualitative review

## Scope and interpretation

Inspected the actual reference primary, secondary and interior PNGs, and the actual primary, secondary, interior and heldout PNGs for anonymous candidates A and B. Read the supplied review instructions and reference brief. Findings were written and frozen before any candidate identity was supplied. No candidate source, implementation records, sibling evidence, other review or internet material informed these findings.

The reference is an offline-lit low-poly scene, not a pixel-level realtime target. Differences in bounce, exposure, background value, noise and shadow softness are described only as appearance differences. This review does not establish their cause. A visual resemblance also cannot establish source structure, editability, mesh topology or correctness of hidden construction.

## Overall finding

Both candidates retain the recognizable open-front repair hall, rear roof, arch, lathe, mezzanine and stairs, yard props, articulated crane, suspended ring, bent exhaust and stock-tube rack. Their primary and secondary views closely preserve the reference's large-scale composition. Their heldout views continue to read as coherent spatial arrangements: the crane becomes nearly edge-on, the side opening reveals the stairs and structure, and the foreground props overlap appropriately.

The clearest construction differences concern the crate arrangements, roof framing, side-opening surround, and some machinery proportions. A has a conspicuous striped or serrated appearance along the arch's exposed inner reveal. B has a more prominent pale rectangular surround around the side window and a brighter, visually larger teal block on the crane base. Both show visibly jagged edges along fine trim and hard ground shadows. Neither collection of images alone establishes whether the exhaust outlet is a genuinely open hollow shell: its end is very dark and its inner rim is not clearly separated.

## Candidate A

### Composition / construction: partly satisfied

- In primary and secondary, the building footprint, front arch, left-hand service door, exposed front roof members, rear roof cover, cart and barrels, crane and pipe rack occupy the expected areas. The proportions of the large architectural masses closely follow the reference.
- The interior view preserves the low frontal composition: the lathe sits inside the arch, the mezzanine and guard are above it, the cart and barrels remain outside, and the suspended ring and portable lamp sit to the right.
- The crate row is longer and more tightly subdivided into narrow upright cases than the reference grouping. The cart's orange tray is explicitly rimmed and its vise is more angular and segmented than the reference appearance. These are visible modeling/styling differences, not evidence of a functional defect.
- The lathe is recognizable, but the very dark, bulky headstock and angular bed details give it a different silhouette from the reference interior view.
- The roof remains visibly open over the service court. Exposed members and ridge details differ in silhouette from the reference, but no added front roof panel obscures the intended opening.

### Three-dimensional consistency / occlusion: satisfied

- The primary view shows the lathe through the front arch and stairs through the side opening. In secondary the stairs move into view behind the right side of the arch while the lathe is partly occluded by the left edge. These changing overlaps support a coherent interior volume.
- In heldout the side aperture exposes stair treads and crossing structural members, while the near side wall hides other interior areas. The arch is seen obliquely with a visible reveal, rather than merely maintaining its frontal outline.
- The crane's arms visually collapse toward an edge-on stack in heldout; the suspended load is partly hidden behind the mechanism. The lamp, cart, barrels, rack and crane base retain plausible spatial relationships across the views.
- No obvious view-dependent disappearance or mutually incompatible placement is apparent in this image set. Exact transforms, articulation constraints and source hierarchy remain unverifiable from still images.

### Curved / hollow forms: partly satisfied

- The front portal reads as a rounded arch with thickness and an unobstructed lower passage. Its opening contains different visible objects as the camera moves.
- A's exposed arch reveal has a repeated light/dark striped edge, especially on the left in primary and interior and on the near reveal in heldout. This makes the opening look rougher and more segmented than the reference. The visible effect is clear; its cause is not established here.
- The suspended ring has a clearly visible central opening and a faceted, thick outer body in primary, secondary and interior. Its thickness and overlap with the cable/hook assembly remain plausible across views.
- The stock tubes show distinct circular rims and dark interiors. Their stacked hollow-cylinder reading survives the heldout angle.
- The exhaust bends convincingly from a vertical riser into a faceted horizontal outlet. The outlet end is nearly uniformly black, most clearly in heldout; a separate inner rim or cavity depth cannot be confidently read. This is insufficient evidence to declare it capped or genuinely open. The concealed inlet is also unverifiable.

### Material / light intent: satisfied

- Ochre architecture, pale arch/trim and concrete, teal equipment, orange and saffron crane members, and dark roof/steel regions remain visually distinct.
- A warm luminous patch appears around the front wall fixture, and another warm patch is visible at the rear through the arch. The portable lamp has a pale cool-white diffuser visibly distinct from its dark hood, thin pole and base, with a cooler pale area around the stand.
- Main cast shadows run consistently across the yard and help attach the crane, rack and cart to the ground. The candidate's harder, more graphic shadows, darker background and stronger local wall glow differ from the reference's softer, noisier image, without constituting a proven rendering fault.
- Dark machinery regions lose some separation, particularly in the primary-view rack and lathe. Thin trim, roof edges and ground shadows have visible stair-stepping or serration. These reduce visual finish, but do not establish which process produced them.
- Whether only opaque fixture parts cast shadows, whether the light is positioned exactly at the diffuser, and the physical material parameters are unverifiable from these views.

## Candidate B

### Composition / construction: partly satisfied

- Primary and secondary preserve the reference's dominant building, arch, service door, yard arrangement and crane silhouette. Interior retains the frontal lathe/mezzanine relationship and the recognizable suspended ring beside the portal.
- The front roof court is visibly uncovered, with a covered rear portion. B's exposed trusses have particularly conspicuous upright center members and an angular raised silhouette, differing from the reference's more visually restrained framing in the supplied views.
- The crate grouping has broader box faces and a different arrangement from the reference's narrow upright grouping. The pale perimeter stepping slabs also appear broader in secondary.
- The side opening has a substantial pale rectangular surround, especially evident in heldout and at the right edge of interior. The reference's side-window trim is slimmer and less visually enclosing. This changes the side elevation's character without preventing the view into the stairs.
- The crane-base equipment block is bright teal and more prominent in the interior and heldout silhouettes than in the reference. The lathe retains its main structural parts but has simplified, sharply squared forms and different small bed/headstock details.

### Three-dimensional consistency / occlusion: satisfied

- The lathe, mezzanine and stairs are visible through the front or side openings in the expected changing combinations. In secondary, the left portal edge masks much of the lathe while the staircase occupies the back-right interior.
- Heldout reveals an oblique arch, a side window containing layered stairs and structural members, a strongly foreshortened crane, and a partly hidden load. This remains consistent with the other views rather than reading as a front-facing arrangement reproduced at a new angle.
- The pipe rack, crane base, lamp, crates and cart maintain plausible relative positions and ground contact. The cart's lower shelf, wheels and upper surface remain spatially legible.
- No obvious mutually incompatible overlap or camera-dependent collapse is visible. Hidden connections, exact articulation constraints and source construction remain unverifiable.

### Curved / hollow forms: partly satisfied

- The portal has a clear rounded opening and visible edge thickness. Its silhouette is generally clean, although fine serration is visible along the right interior trim and under horizontal architectural edges.
- The ring has a readable central hole and faceted body, with a visible cable/hook connection. It remains appropriately occluded when the crane turns nearly edge-on in heldout.
- The stock tubes retain clear rims and dark bores across primary, secondary and heldout. They read as open tubes rather than solid logs.
- The exhaust has a continuous faceted bend and distinct vertical and horizontal runs. As with A, the dark end face does not clearly reveal a separate inner rim or cavity depth. Outlet and concealed inlet openness cannot be established from these images; the dark face alone is not proof of a cap.

### Material / light intent: satisfied

- Warm ochre/sand architecture, teal machinery, orange/saffron crane members, dark roof/steel and pale concrete remain distinct. B's teal crane-base block is notably brighter than adjacent steel and than its counterpart in the reference.
- The front and rear warm fixtures produce clear warm wall patches. The portable lamp's bright pale diffuser remains visibly separate from a dark hood and pole, with a cooler pale ground area near the stand. The intended warm/cool separation is readable.
- Cast shadows are spatially coherent and anchor the main objects. Shadows around the pipe rack and along the slab are hard and visibly jagged compared with the reference's softer treatment. Some fine dark/light edge breakup is also visible around the crane elbow and architectural trim.
- These appearance differences do not establish a causal rendering limitation or an implementation error. Static views cannot verify the diffuser's shadow participation, exact source placement or material settings.

## Limits and useful distinctions

A's arch reveal is the more conspicuously striped visible surface. B's pale side-opening surround, center-post roof framing, broader crate faces and bright crane-base block are the more conspicuous construction/style departures. Both retain the scene's main spatial logic and material/light vocabulary. Both leave the exhaust's actual hollow construction unresolved by the available pixels.

The heldout camera has no matching reference image. It is used here only to examine spatial continuity and occlusion, not to demand unseen reference details. No numeric score, implementation identity, productivity judgment or causal engine comparison is assigned.
