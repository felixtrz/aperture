# Independent qualitative image review

## Scope and material findings

Actual pixels were inspected with `view_image` for all eight candidate captures and both reference images listed below. X/Y identities remained blinded. This is exploratory qualitative evidence, without scores, rankings, engine attribution, or a formal acceptance verdict.

- No obvious large missing face, frame-edge clipping, broken boom joint, or detached crane component was visible in the eight captures. All scenes fit comfortably inside the image. Small or hidden surface defects remain outside what these views can establish.
- The arch reads as an open passage in every capture. The pipe's visible mouths are dark, rimmed openings rather than visibly sealed caps. The rear-quarter views show both pipe ends; the high-oblique views mainly establish the upward-facing opening.
- In both rear-quarter-all captures, the wall cap/pillar occludes the lower cable and hook region. This is a material visibility limitation, not evidence of a disconnected load. Both high-oblique-all captures show a visually continuous boom-tip cable, hook region, and crate straps.
- In both rear-quarter-all captures, the enlarged pipe's lower mouth meets or overlaps the platform silhouette near x545–580, y565–590. The screenshots alone cannot establish pipe/platform clearance or penetration.
- X captures have broad, very soft cast shadows. Y captures have clearly visible stippled shadow boundaries and speckling on several shadowed/contact regions. The floor-shadow directions remain broadly coherent within each viewpoint. No isolated obviously reversed cast shadow was identified.
- Fine light edge traces are visible on X rear-quarter platform tiers and some arch/wall contours. They do not visibly expose a large background-colored hole; their status as ordinary edge contrast versus a seam cannot be determined from these stills.

Coordinates below are approximate pixels in the 1024 × 1024 captures, measured from the upper-left corner. They identify locations, not a quantitative image analysis.

## Pose continuity and acceptance limits

Within each X/Y pair, the baseline and all captures show the expected direction of the stated design changes: altered arm articulation, load position, wider arch, larger pipe bend, thicker top tier, and changed platform/crane orientation and placement. Visible components continue to read as one assembled scene. The exact angles, dimensions, hoist travel, yaw, and translation cannot be measured reliably from the screenshots and were not verified. A changed projection or intentional pose change is not treated as a defect.

The eight stills support only visible-surface observations at two viewpoints and two poses. They do not prove watertightness, hidden-face correctness, exact joint coincidence, absence of intersections, stable animation, shadow correctness at other light settings, or equivalent behavior outside these captures. The reference images differ in appearance and are not an equality target. Formal benchmark gates remain missing. No pass/fail acceptance or implementation diagnosis is warranted here.

## Most useful bounded next diagnostic

Request one tightly framed, fixed-pose pair of additional stills of the all configuration from a modestly shifted rear-quarter angle that separates the wall cap from the hook and separates the enlarged pipe's lower rim from the platform. Keep pose and lighting unchanged. This would establish whether the suspension connection is continuous when exposed and whether the pipe/platform overlap is only projection. If clearance remains hidden, a separately authorized geometric clearance check would be needed. This recommendation was not executed.
