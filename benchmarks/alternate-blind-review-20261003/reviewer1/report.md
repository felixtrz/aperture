# Independent exploratory visual review

## Scope and outcome

Actual pixels were inspected for all eight candidate captures and both supplied reference PNGs. X/Y identities remained blinded. This is a qualitative screenshot review, with no scores, ranking, engine identification, or root-cause diagnosis. The references were treated as baseline design intent, not pixel-equality targets.

Across the visible surfaces, there is no unmistakable missing face, open geometry tear, detached major crane member, or object cut off by the image boundary. The arch remains visibly open, the teal elbow pipe reads as hollow, and the suspended crate reads as attached to the crane in the high-oblique views. These observations do not establish watertightness, collision freedom, mechanically correct hidden joints, temporal continuity, or formal benchmark acceptance.

The material visible concerns are fine edge ambiguity at foundation tiers and stippled shadow/surface appearance in the Y captures. Rear-quarter all-pose views also have an important visibility limitation: the wall cap hides the lower hoist and hook. That is camera occlusion, not evidence of a broken cable.

Coordinates below are approximate pixels from the top-left of the 1024 by 1024 images.

## Acceptance limitations and bounded next diagnostic

- Static screenshots establish endpoint appearance only. They cannot verify motion continuity between poses, numerical transform values, hidden attachment integrity, collision freedom, mesh topology or watertightness.
- Some parts occupy only a few pixels, and the wall cap hides the all-pose hook in both rear-quarter captures. The high-oblique captures reduce, but do not eliminate, that uncertainty.
- Fine foundation-edge lines are visible, but there is insufficient evidence to label them unintended cracks. The images show no broad see-through opening there.
- Stippled shadow/surface appearance is directly visible in the Y set. A still image alone cannot establish flicker, convergence behavior, sampling cause or a benchmark defect.
- Formal benchmark gates remain missing. This review provides no numerical or formal acceptance result.

The most useful bounded next diagnostic is a focused close-up of the already identified crane attachment and front/right platform-tier boundaries at the same two endpoint poses, with camera visibility sufficient to expose the hidden hook and shoulder support contacts. The specific questions are whether the light tier strips are real openings or exposed ledges, and whether the occluded joints meet. If the stippled appearance is acceptance-relevant, repeat the exact Y camera/pose once with unchanged settings to determine whether the observed pattern changes. These are recommendations only; no additional capture, browser session or test was performed for this review.
